## Hypothesis and acceptance

Earlier random mixed-color tails barely improved choice frequency. A five-star shower can instead use three arrivals to build a group and two to support a different existing family. Hypothesis: this offers more simultaneous groups without returning long dry stretches or making idle play safe. Before native work, compare broad pairing against pairing only natural five-star showers while preserving dry-board assistance. Check the opening, dry waits, score/survival, clear-all behavior, pressure choices, old saves, announced previews and fixed challenge behavior.

Adoption additionally needs a native playthrough and observed comprehension/replay preference; a bot score alone does not decide. This tranche produces a playable comparison, not adoption into the default game. Keep it through the next paired observed-play session or explicit mechanics decision, then adopt or remove it. The higher-column refinement below remains offline.

## Exact candidate

The natural-only patch applies to new survival skies when a natural five-star shower occurs with an existing legal group and enough space. Three matching stars target a lowest eligible column with height at most four. If another column has a colored top, a different family and height at most five, the last two stars support that top family in a lowest eligible column. Otherwise the tail keeps the original rule. Dusk still replaces its scheduled arrival. There is no extra star, power-up, cascade, score bonus or new input.

Opening and dry-board assistance remain unchanged. First Light does not use paired arrivals. New lab saves store an optional generation marker; missing markers retain the current generator, including current cohesive saves. A contradictory marker is invalid. Clears and balance changes do not rewrite announced previews. No additional RNG draw is introduced directly, but changed boards can alter later relief/count branches and therefore future RNG consumption; equal seeds do not imply identical later skies across variants.

This intentionally biases actual landing columns. The original column bag is still consumed, but no longer guarantees landing fairness for paired showers. That is an experimental tradeoff to inspect, not an unchanged contract. Candidate saves belong only to their lab; there is no supported import into the production app or between candidate rules.

## Results

All samples use seeds 0–199, six policies and a 60-shower cap. Totals for surviving rounds are censored, not completed-round scores. One-clear policies are diagnostic constraints; clear-all removes every available group.

| Clear-all metric | Baseline | Pair all five-star showers | Natural-only pairing |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Multiple-group decision states | 6.7% | 38.3% | 11.6% |
| Mean showers observed | 26.45 | 55.07 | 32.27 |
| Rounds ended within cap | 200/200 | 80/200 | 197/200 |
| Longest dry run | 1 shower | 1 shower | 1 shower |

Broad pairing is rejected for the next native comparison: it greatly extends clear-all survival and obscures pressure. Natural-only pairing is the playable candidate. Under the first-group policy, choice frequency rises from 9.6% to 18.9%, but first/largest/pressure scores still differ little. More groups do not prove meaningful strategy. Under clear-all, high-column alternatives rise only from 114/3224 to 168/4337 decisions. The selected sample therefore improves opportunity variety more clearly than strategic consequence.

An offline refinement targets the **highest eligible** primary column (still height at most four) instead of the lowest. `pressure.json` records it: clear-all choice frequency is 12.0%, all 200 rounds end within the cap, and high-column alternatives occur at 262/4210 decisions. This is a reason to compare pressure targeting next, not to switch the native candidate without a playthrough. One canonical natural-only patch owns all shared rules; strict primary-column/eligibility substitutions derive the two diagnostic variants. The runner fails when its unique transformation anchor changes, and reports the derived core hash. Its generator is a separate candidate, with no cross-candidate save migration.

## Limits and next decision

No human preference, actual VoiceOver speech/focus, physical-device feel/profile, minimum-OS or tablet acceptance is established. Still screenshots are not full-animation evidence. The renderer, feedback and lifecycle code are unchanged in the shower lab; hosted tests cover their coordination, not visual quality. Compare natural-only and the pressure hypothesis with the unchanged baseline before adopting a generator. Inspect whether the player can name a useful choice, whether repeated targeting feels predictable, and whether clearing a high column helps. The existing falling-sky playtest protocol remains authoritative for observed play.

Research method follows the earlier [matching-tile automated-playtesting paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.06570): use several policies as diagnostics and preserve a separate human-observation boundary. This paper does not endorse either candidate or validate these simple policies as player models.
