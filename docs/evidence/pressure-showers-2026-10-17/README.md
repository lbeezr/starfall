## Hypothesis and criteria

Targeting the highest eligible column in natural five-star paired showers may create useful pressure decisions while retaining early opportunity variety. Preserve opening, Dusk, fixed First Light, preview and old-save contracts. Compare all 200 seeds under the existing six policies, including clear-all. Verify actual native clears and their next-shower outcomes; verify the single-deadline store makes a first choice matter when rain becomes due during presentation. A native fixture is a diagnostic example, not proof of enjoyment or a universal strategy.

The shared `shower_candidate.py` now owns variant derivation for both the sampler and exporter, using the same canonical patch. Existing natural/broad/pressure metrics remained identical after extraction. Height and squared-height pressure definitions are reused from the pinned sampler when searching for a decision fixture. Each lab has a distinct bundle/defaults suite; cross-candidate save imports are unsupported. The existing optional generation marker preserves continuation of baseline saves within each candidate. This coverage does not establish legacy-generation selection when starting a new replay round; adoption must specify and test replay/restart generation policy as well as restore.

## Observations

Under the one-clear first-group policy, both natural-only and pressure variants offer a first choice by shower twenty in **160/200** rounds, versus **108/200** for baseline. Clear-all: pressure 158/200, natural-only 156/200, baseline 105/200. These are shower indices, not measured player times. `choiceAfter20` and `noChoice` distinguish late observations from no observed choice before overflow/the sixty-shower cap. Do not infer enjoyment from those counts.

The pressure fixture is seed 12, shower 37, score 99. The aqua group at core cell `(3,2)` (spoken row 4, column 3) collects four starlight and touches a seven-high column. The rose group at `(3,5)` (row 4, column 6) collects three. After one clear followed by the already announced shower, aqua survives; rose overflows. Post-clear squared-height pressure is 142 versus 168. Both actual scores are asserted natively before advancing, preventing an invalid tap from falsely passing the overflow case.

This is not an irreversible wrong-move puzzle. While paused, the player can clear both groups. A new hosted check advances the injected automatic clock to 3.9 seconds, clears either group, lets rain become due at four seconds, and verifies the second clear cannot preempt due rain. Completing presentation delivers exactly one shower: aqua survives, rose loses. This establishes the consequence at the existing input/clock boundary. It does not introduce a move limit or delay the clock.

The safer group also pays more, so this example does not demonstrate a score-versus-survival tradeoff. Landing columns remain hidden; the observer knows the counterfactual outcome, while the player sees column pressure and the existing preview. Human comprehension and preference remain necessary.
