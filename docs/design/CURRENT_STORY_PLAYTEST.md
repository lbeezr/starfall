## A five-minute first pilot

Use the build/round identification below, then allow about three minutes of natural Lantern play and two minutes of discussion, extending only if the player wants. Record the first intentional clear, one intended setup and its actual number of exchanges when observable, a satisfying or frustrating moment and what the player chooses to do next. Ask for informal satisfaction and sense-of-control ratings separately, each 1–5, with a concrete moment explaining each answer. These are local descriptive prompts, not a validated scale or a population estimate.

Distinguish thinking through a desired setup from searching without a plan, unwanted waiting and unsuccessful intended input. A setup exchange that earns no immediate points can still be satisfying. Record an actual recovery episode only if one occurs. Do not force a completed round to fit five minutes, or classify continuing an unfinished round as replay.

The earlier seven-versus-four line comparison is historical preparation, not a treatment in this five-player packet. Current new Lantern/Ribbon Falling Sky visits already use revision 4. Keep builds, modes and familiarity separate rather than pooling old comparison visits into these first-session observations.

## Time the first understood clear without coaching

The [player-momentum plan](https://github.com/lbeezr/starfall/issues/393) proposes an understood first clear within **30 seconds of Play** as an initial design target. Do not tell the player this target, display a countdown, hurry them or stop the session at 30 seconds. Record elapsed time descriptively; this small sample does not establish a universal onboarding threshold.

| Event | Record consistently |
| --- | --- |
| Home discovery | Whether the player finds and chooses **Play Lantern Landing**, another ordinary mode or no start; any hesitation and help. Keep this interval separate from Play-to-clear time. |
| Start | The actual tap on **Play Lantern Landing** in a candidate-containing build, or ordinary **Begin** otherwise; record which start and do not pool them. Include the ordinary handoff, thinking, inputs and animation; do not start when the board settles or subtract delays afterward. Record timing method and uncertainty. |
| First visible award | Time and visible cause: player exchange, automatic shower or unknown. A quick automatic award is not evidence of an intentional clear. |
| First intentional clear | Time when a pattern deliberately arranged by the player first visibly resolves. Record the chosen action and evidence of intention; spontaneous words are useful. If intent was not stated, ask afterward, “What were you trying to do, and what happened?” Keep their explanation separate from the observed action. A lucky result or uncertain intention stays separate. |
| Understanding and satisfaction | After that action or at the natural stop, record whether they can explain the matching result, whether it felt satisfying and why. A correctly repeated cue alone does not establish understanding or satisfaction. |
| Missing or assisted timing | Record **not reached** or **not measurable**, the last observed time and reason. If factual help or a demonstration occurs, retain the elapsed time with exact assistance; it cannot count as an uncoached-by-moderator result. Do not replace an unfinished attempt with a coached restart. |

Defer rule questions and “What are you considering?” prompts until the first player-caused clear or a natural stop, unless the player asks for help. Do not request think-aloud explanations during the initial timed interval. Record spontaneous remarks. Any interruption, prompt, pause or held contact remains in the record, including its effect on automatic play.

## Mode and return observation

After the first uncoached exposure, ask: “What were you trying to do, what happened to the stars you chose or arranged, and what would you choose next?” If the player visits Home, Help, Continue or Replay, ask what they expect that action to keep or replace before they use it. Record their words and actual result separately, including the installed build, selected mode, assistance and whether the round was new, retained or replayed. Do not introduce drawing to Story or call an unfinished visit a replay. Leave this blank until observed; simulator journeys do not answer it.

Use “What would you like to do now?” once, with real time to choose. Record the actual action separately from the answer: continue the unfinished round, independently start Replay/new visit, browse, stop, or unavailable time. A moderator-directed start, promised future play and a new round started only to complete a test are separate from voluntary continuation. If there was no available time or observed choice, mark that opportunity **not assessed**.

## Watch before teaching

Opening: “We’re trying the game, not testing you. Play in whatever way makes sense to you. You can pause or stop whenever you want.” Ask about preferred access settings without requesting a diagnosis. Allow breaks and unhurried reading; never force an ending.

1. **Uncoached entry and play.** Let the player start and play without demonstrating a move or asking rule questions before the timed clear. Record first intended action, cue use, useful setups, waiting, mistaken tap/trace expectations and naturally occurring chains/lines. Ask about rule/goal interpretation afterward. Do not label a missed opportunity unless the before-state establishes that one existed.
2. **A later natural decision.** After the initial timing interval, if useful, ask once, “What are you considering?” Record any prediction before input and the committed result afterward. Distinguish spontaneous explanation, neutral prompt, factual help and a strategy demonstration. Asking during automatic play changes the exposure: note the prompt, pause or held-contact interval. If no prediction was given, do not infer one from the result.
3. **Recovery and ending.** Observe a real mistaken setup, crowded column or blocked arrival if one occurs. Ask afterward what changed and what they might try next. Do not manufacture a mistake, loss, chain or full line. Record unfinished rounds, score and stop reason; unfinished is not lost. Record result comprehension separately from control comprehension.
4. **Debrief and choice.** Ask what felt satisfying, confusing or repetitive. Record satisfaction and perceived control separately, each 1–5 with one concrete moment and any disagreement. Use the mode/return observation above for the next actual choice; do not repeatedly ask to obtain a continuation.
5. **After the baseline only:** if the player is willing, observe Pause/resume, Settings dismissal and a brief background/return through normal controls. Note the visible score/board/forecast before and after and any unexpected advance, lost intent or stale feedback. Keep these prompted checks separate from natural play. Help, demonstrations or practice can follow; they cannot become an uncoached repeat.

Plan five **distinct first-time people**, with both device classes represented. Previous Starfall exposure, including Tutorial/help or play on another device, makes the visit a familiar exposure; keep it separately rather than filling a first-time slot. Help first requested during this visit remains part of that person's first session, marked assisted. If one person switches devices, the second device is a familiar session, not another person. Record device and location order. Keep phone/tablet, first exposure/returning, new/replayed, automatic/paused, different builds and changed settings separate. Ask the player to use their usual motion/text/input setup; do not force reduced motion for a favorable result. Prepared positions or Tutorial examples belong after natural play, in separately marked records.
