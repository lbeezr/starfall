## Watch before teaching

Opening: “We’re trying the game, not testing you. Play in whatever way makes sense to you. You can pause or stop whenever you want.” Ask about preferred access settings without requesting a diagnosis. Allow breaks and unhurried reading; never force an ending.

1. **Uncoached entry and play.** Ask “What do you think you can do, and what are you trying to achieve?” Let the player start and play without demonstrating a move. Record rule/goal interpretation, first intended action, useful setups, waiting, mistaken tap/trace expectations and naturally occurring chains/lines. Do not label a missed opportunity unless the before-state establishes that one existed.
2. **A natural decision.** If useful, ask once, “What are you considering?” Record any prediction before input and the committed result afterward. Distinguish spontaneous explanation, neutral prompt, factual help and a strategy demonstration. Asking during automatic play changes the exposure: note the prompt, pause or held-contact interval. If no prediction was given, do not infer one from the result.
3. **Recovery and ending.** Observe a real mistaken setup, crowded column or blocked arrival if one occurs. Ask afterward what changed and what they might try next. Do not manufacture a mistake, loss, chain or full line. Record unfinished rounds, score and stop reason; unfinished is not lost. Record result comprehension separately from control comprehension.
4. **Debrief and choice.** Ask what felt satisfying, confusing or repetitive, then “What would you like to do now?” Record stated preference separately from an actual independent replay/new-visit start, a directed start, continuing an unfinished round or stopping. No available time means no assessed replay opportunity.
5. **After the baseline only:** if the player is willing, observe Pause/resume, Settings dismissal and a brief background/return through normal controls. Note the visible score/board/forecast before and after and any unexpected advance, lost intent or stale feedback. Keep these prompted checks separate from natural play. Help, demonstrations or practice can follow; they cannot become an uncoached repeat.

Obtain baseline observations on **both device classes**. Prefer different first-time people when possible; if one person switches devices, the second device is a familiar session. Record device and location order. Keep phone/tablet, first exposure/returning, new/replayed, automatic/paused, different builds and changed settings separate. Ask the player to use their usual motion/text/input setup; do not force reduced motion for a favorable result. Prepared positions or Tutorial examples belong after natural play, in separately marked records.
