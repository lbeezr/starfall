# Design stages around what the stars restore

**Status:** Draft · **Owner:** Current implementer · **Updated:** October 18, 2026

**Chosen foundation:** Murk, the Hungry Moon, and all twelve map locations are user-approved. The rewritten [game bible](STARFALL_GAME_BIBLE.md) owns the lore, cast and story; the [world atlas](WORLD_ATLAS.md) develops each place. This document owns the proposed connection between committed starlight, visible restoration and the level win.

**Recommendation:** Give each level one announced starlight objective. Its completion restores a recognizable local purpose. Intermediate states, targets and pressure need playable validation; the former Observatory-first proposal and 6/12/18 milestones are superseded.

**Next action:** Develop Lantern Landing at 0/25/50/75/100% with the real cast, then make one bounded playable comparison. These are art checkpoints, not fixed production thresholds. [Experiment status](../EXPERIMENTS.md) owns adoption; current runtime environments, First Light and saves remain unchanged.

## Start with a purpose worth bringing back

Working premise: **Help the little stars bring forgotten pockets of the universe back to life.** A stage brief should answer what is missing, how the stars help, and what becomes possible at completion. The initial scene must suggest its potential, and the finished scene must make the accomplishment recognizable.

For example, reconnecting a bridge lets travelers cross; awakening a seed moon gives a tiny world somewhere to grow; restoring a sky choir brings a silent region back into harmony. These are exploratory concepts, not a selected catalog or commitments to three new environments.

Keep the gentle character of Starfall while exploring more than dimness: separated pieces, interrupted flow, dormant life, lost rhythm or a structure missing its purpose. Literal damage and darkness are options. The playable board and controls must remain legible from the beginning.

## A light thief gives the restoration a cause

The user's proposed villain makes the starting state a consequence of something that happened here. The place once had life and purpose; someone carried its light away. The little stars arrive afterward, and the player's clears help return it. This supplies a simple story through the same actions and transformations that communicate progress.

The [approved Murk sheet and story](STARFALL_GAME_BIBLE.md#murk-the-hungry-moon) replace earlier dark-star/comet/cloud comparisons. Preserve his moon body, expressive hands, smoky tail and captured colors. The user explicitly chose him as made; the separate hero question does not reopen this design.

For the first storyboard, show the light theft through one brief entrance/action/exit, then local returns of color/life as the player helps. The completed stage regains its particular function. A shared trace could eventually connect stages into a journey, but does not require a campaign or a boss fight in the first comparison. How the story ultimately resolves remains open.

Make the initial state wistful and visibly diminished: muted environmental colors, folded forms, still mechanisms and sparse sound are possible cues. Restoration can return these at different moments rather than brighten every layer together. Preserve enough detail to suggest what was lost. Keep playable stars, their family cues, text and controls readable throughout; avoid desaturating the entire screen as one image.

The [bible](STARFALL_GAME_BIBLE.md#the-star-cast-carries-the-journey) develops Dusk as a friendly cast member and stars as willing light-sharing helpers. Existing Dusk rules remain a separate runtime contract; no villain-minion link or sacrifice is implied by restoration.

### Kamek reference: a brief entrance with a clear consequence

The user identified Kamek's brief flights into/out of an area and visible acts of magic as the performance reference. Nintendo's [Woolly World description](https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/Games/Wii-U-games/Yoshi-s-Woolly-World-892537.html) confirms the broader narrative pattern: Kamek transforms most Yoshis into yarn, giving the remaining Yoshis a rescue objective. This research read the official description; it did not independently review footage or establish shot timing. The brief staging reference comes from the user's description.

Design inference: give our light thief intention, expression and a recognizable movement signature. A dark comet could itself be expressive, or could describe how a character travels. A passing effect alone may not supply the recurring personality the reference suggests. Murk’s approved sheet supplies the chosen silhouette and acting vocabulary.

One proposed vignette, to test in a few seconds:

| Beat | Action | What it communicates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrive | A distinctive silhouette swoops in and notices a source of light | This is a recognizable visitor with intent |
| Take | One clear gesture pulls light and color out of the place | We see who caused the problem and what was taken |
| React | The thief admires the stolen glow, with a small smug or comic expression | A moment of personality makes the action memorable |
| Leave | The thief flies away with the light; the quiet place and little stars remain | The player has a clear restoration job and space to begin |

Borrow this compact performance structure while designing our own silhouette, movement, theft gesture and sound. Keep the action understandable without dialogue or audio. Begin with a gently theatrical troublemaker as a tone hypothesis; the bible now develops Murk’s possessive appetite as the writing direction.

Use appearances at entry or other deliberate story moments. Active puzzle play should remain uninterrupted in the first candidate. Retry/resume should reach play promptly; skipping or quiet modes should show the same settled stage and objective. An optional later reaction to a restored place can build continuity without taking the player's earned restoration away. Do not assume a scene at every level or milestone, a boss system, or random mid-round sabotage.

If implemented, the vignette presents the authored initial condition. Completion, skipping, backgrounding or replaying its animation must not change the board, score, saves or shower schedule. Existing explicit Begin/resume and cancellation boundaries still apply.

## The stage from 0% to 100%

| Review state | What the player should see | Design question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **0% — a clear need** | A recognizable place with something visibly interrupted or missing; the light-thief concept leaves muted color and traces of passage, with hints of what the place could become | Can I understand what the stars are here to help? |
| **25% — the first response** | A local repair or awakening directly connected to a clear; one part starts working | Can I see that my action made a difference? |
| **50% — a meaningful change** | Previously isolated parts connect, motion returns or life spreads through the scene | Does this place behave differently now? |
| **75% — the finish is in sight** | Most of the place works; the remaining gap or dormant centerpiece is visible | Can I anticipate what finishing will accomplish? |
| **100% — purpose restored** | One distinctive final event completes the job, followed by a satisfying settled scene | Can I recognize the win even without the result label? |

Use small continuous responses between major changes so ordinary clears still feel useful. Several authored states can sit on top of continuous committed progress; we do not need 101 separate backgrounds. Checkpoints need not be equally spaced in the playable design. Avoid front-loading every interesting change and leaving a long wait for completion.

Percentages describe completion of this stage's announced job, not screen brightness or completion of an entire world. The player-facing HUD might use a light count, percentage or another clear progress cue; that presentation is still open. Lighting, structure, movement, life and sound can change independently. A quiet night scene can be fully restored at 100%.

## Transformation variety behind the stage proposal

The examples below motivated contrasting repair, awakening and harmony. The approved twelve-location direction and all sixty proposed state notes now live in the [world package](world-01/README.md); these earlier examples are not a separate candidate list.

| Exploratory idea | 0% | 50% | 100% payoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Broken star bridge — reconnect** | A graceful arc is split into drifting sections, with a destination visible beyond | Sections align and paths of light connect some spans | The final span joins; a small procession crosses and the distant destination answers |
| **Sleeping seed moon — awaken** | A curled seed rests on a small bare moon, with hints of roots beneath the surface | Roots reconnect and shoots unfold across the moon | The central seed opens into a luminous canopy, sheltering the little stars |
| **Silent sky choir — restore harmony** | Suspended bell-like forms hang out of alignment, their light patterns interrupted | Restored forms sway and answer each other in visible pulses | The ensemble produces a brief shared phrase and settles into a gentle rhythm; the visual completion works with sound off |

Judge each idea by how clearly it communicates need, how interesting its middle states are, and how distinct its ending feels. Also compare board readability, art/animation cost and whether the final event remains pleasant on replay. A beautiful completed painting alone is insufficient to choose a stage.

## How restoration defines the ending

The initial candidate uses one announced starlight objective. A clear contributes directly to it. When committed progress reaches the target, the stage is complete: showers stop, the final clear resolves, and the scene presents its final transformation. There is no hidden extra task at 100%, exact-score requirement or requirement to empty the board. Keep replay/new-round actions explicit and available without waiting through a long ceremony.

For this candidate, normalized progress is collected starlight divided by the authored target, capped at completion. Keep the uncapped score. Core owns the result; the environment presents it. A rounded HUD must not announce 100% before the goal actually commits. One large clear can cross several milestones; settle all the resulting changes without forcing a chain of cutscenes.

Choose the target and pressure after specifying the stage's purpose and testing useful puzzle decisions. First Light's 18 is an existing technical baseline, not a target for every stage. Retain overflow as the initial failure constraint for comparison, with pause/manual play supported. Stage-specific goals, altered rules or a different failure model need an explicit next design rather than being inferred from a prop.

Restoration is direct feedback for the round's progress. No spend screen, repair currency or required prop tapping is proposed. Do not divide light among several independent errands in the first test. The win condition should stay easy to explain while the transformation becomes richer.

## The map proposal

A future map could show the consequences of completed work: a bridge reconnects regions, life returns to a moon, a silent area answers its neighbors. Each stage still needs a local, understandable finish. Region-wide recovery across several levels would require a separate progress definition.

The user has approved all twelve locations across the illustrated map. The [world design](STARFALL_GAME_BIBLE.md#the-twelve-location-journey) recommends a clear main route, persistent completed places and explicit replay reenactments. These are design recommendations; no world save format or unlock behavior is implemented. On failure, avoid visually destroying the player's repairs or blaming the cast. Test persistence and retry semantics before adopting them.

## Production boundaries

The [current nightscape contract](environments/night-production-09/README.md) owns native scenery boundaries. The [earlier environment production process](environments/PRODUCTION.md) retains historical composition and acceptance guidance. Future restoration stages require explicit composition, geometry, asset and lifecycle checks. Extend its [brief](environments/LOCATION_BRIEF.md) with the chosen transformation and state sequence before making final art.

- Derive scene state from committed progress. Restoring a save shows the correct settled stage; background, settings, restart and scene replacement cancel stale effects. Animation callbacks never award light, schedule showers or determine completion.
- Keep geometry and input stable through the restoration. For the first candidate, scenery changes do not move the playable board, change hit targets or consume gameplay RNG. Any later geometry proposal needs its own native mapping checks.
- Use independently authored elements for structural changes and light. Preserve faces, silhouettes, non-color cues and text contrast at every state; darkness is environmental mood, not reduced usability.
- Calm effects and Reduce Motion retain the same completed scene with reduced transitions. Progress and results have text/native semantics; sound and color never carry the only completion cue. Follow the [development/RC accessibility cadence](../DEVELOPMENT_BUILDS.md#accessibility-checks-by-build-stage).

## Acceptance and decision points

Hypothesis: a stage chosen for its restoration arc makes progress and completion more meaningful than the same puzzle with only a counter and ending ceremony. Human observation must assess satisfaction separately from rule correctness.

- [x] **World selection:** The user approved Murk as made and all twelve map locations on October 18. This is creative approval, not native or gameplay acceptance.
- [ ] **First-stage storyboard:** Compare Lantern Landing’s five states. A reviewer can identify the initial need, distinguish the middle states and explain the payoff without labels. The theft communicates what was taken, keeps helpers visibly safe and connects the player’s actions to its return.
- [ ] **Gameplay fit:** Before implementing the selected candidate, specify its target, pressure, save/retry behavior and comparison baseline. A player can identify a useful choice and anticipate the finishing clear. More light collected does not by itself prove skill or enjoyment.
- [ ] **Human comparison:** Compare one bounded stage with and without its restoration presentation, including replay. Record whether the scene adds satisfaction, distracts from play, or loses its appeal on repetition. Counterevidence remains visible.
- [ ] **Visual and motion checks:** Inspect all states with sparse/dense boards, largest text, full/reduced animation and sound off. Verify a large clear crossing several thresholds. A concept strip or still cannot verify runtime motion.
- [ ] **Rules and lifecycle checks:** Verify threshold/overshoot/rounding, terminal lock, partial/completed restore, rapid input, overflow, replay, settings dismissal and background/resume. Source/resource work needs focused regression checks, core and affected native tests, simulator builds and appropriate Release checks.
- [ ] **Adoption decision:** After the selected stage's matched human round/replay comparison and applicable native checks, adopt, revise or remove it. Name the remaining question and next check for any extension. Keep a single bounded candidate rather than accumulating permanent pilot routes.
